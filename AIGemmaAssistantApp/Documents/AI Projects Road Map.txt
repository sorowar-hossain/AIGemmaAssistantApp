
Since you already work with C#, ASP.NET Core, SQL Server, RAG, semantic search, ML.NET, and Gemma, I recommend building one reusable Corporate AI Platform and then developing individual AI applications on top of it.
The goal is to create something your company can demonstrate to management, integrate into existing client systems, and eventually offer as a commercial product.

Corporate AI Development Roadmap
1. The overall development plan
Phase 1 — AI fundamentals and architecture

Week 1–2
Understand LLMs, embeddings, RAG, tool calling, and structured output.
Decide when to use an LLM versus traditional machine learning.
Define privacy, permissions, and evaluation requirements.

Phase 2 — Build shared AI services
Week 3–4
Create an ASP.NET Core Web API.
Build reusable model integration, prompt handling, and embedding services.
Add logging, configuration, error handling, and authentication.

Phase 3 — Develop the first AI application
Week 5–6
Build an Enterprise AI Knowledge Assistant.
Upload sample documents and implement RAG.
Return answers with source references.
Test accuracy and document access permissions.

Phase 4 — Develop additional AI modules
Week 7–10
Intelligent Document Processing.
Customer Support Assistant.
Business Intelligence Assistant.
Reuse shared components wherever possible.

Phase 5 — Advanced AI applications
Week 11–14** - AI Code Review. - Contract and Compliance Analysis.
- Recruitment Matching. - Predictive Analytics and Project Risk Detection.

Phase 6 — Pilot and production readiness
Week 15–16 and ongoing
Deploy to a controlled test environment.
Measure quality, speed, cost, and user satisfaction.
Review security and failure cases.

Collect feedback and prepare a client demonstration.
These timelines are an illustrative plan for a focused prototype effort, not a guarantee. Building all the applications to production quality would require more time, especially for data integration, security, and validation.

Api: HTTP endpoints and authentication integration.
Application: business workflows and orchestration.
Domain: business entities and rules.
Infrastructure: database, files, and external integrations.
AI: reusable model inference, retrieval, and prediction services.
Tests: automated testing and AI quality evaluation.
Start with a modular monolith rather than microservices. You can split components into separate services later if usage, deployment, or scaling requirements justify it.

5. How to measure success
Before calling any AI application complete, define measurable acceptance criteria.
Metric
What to evaluate
Accuracy
Does the application produce correct results on a test set?
Grounding
Can answers be traced to approved source information?
Security
Can users access only the information they are authorized to see?
Performance
What are the response time and throughput under expected load?
Cost
What are the model, infrastructure, and maintenance costs?
Business impact
How much time or manual effort does the solution actually save?
Don't measure success by whether the AI produces fluent answers. Measure whether it reliably solves the intended business problem.
